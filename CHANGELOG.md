# Changelog

## 0.42
- In the alley a heavy hammer comes only for a figure felled with one ball, as on the
  street for a building felled with one throw; the gods drop it only then.
- Lightning is made fresh for every strike: from anywhere behind the thunderer's cloud
  to anywhere below, now and then across to another cloud; a jagged channel with forks
  thinning to their tips; one to four pulses down the same channel; two to four strikes,
  near and thick or far and thin, at uneven intervals; the whole sky flashes with each.
- Thunder of six kinds: a split crack close by, a double crack, a roll in waves, a long
  roll between the houses, a low growl far off, and the old one. The nearer the strike,
  the sooner and the harder it comes; a far one is heard late and low.

## 0.41
- The golem comes apart for real now. After its last leap it sinks lower than a crouch,
  its seams open and it holds a moment; then the shell bursts off it, pieces of its stone,
  brick, planks and iron thrown all round, bouncing once and crumbling to dust, and the
  stones of the figure come out of its own body: from the shins and thighs, the torso,
  the arms, the shoulders and the head, each in an arc, turning upright as it flies. They
  land in a wave from the front of the figure back, each kicking up once with a knock of
  its own, the last one the heaviest. Gathered again, every stone flies back to its own
  place in the body, and the golem closes up from them.
- The golem rocks in its leaps: over onto one side, the other side next time, its arms
  swinging a few frames behind its body. In the street fight too.
- The gods look on in the alley as well: after every figure the view tips up to them,
  delight for one ball, smiles for two, a nod for three, and the heavy hammer comes down;
  a figure left standing gets their gloom before the view turns home.
- The button on the lamp post no longer calls the player on the hundredth level.

## 0.40
- The ball that rolls is the ball that was in hand: the same color, turned as it was held.
  The next one taken up is always another color of the set.

## 0.39
- The turn round to the alley is a turn of the whole body now: three sweeps of the head,
  past the houses at a third of the way and at two thirds, with a nod at each.
- Life behind: people stroll on the pavements down to the shore road and along the beach;
  once the golem has come through, cars pass along the shore road far off.
- The figures are the golem's own stones and as big as it is: pillars twice a man's
  height, walls, gates, pyramids.
- New rules. Three balls to a figure; fell most of it (the bar shows what still stands,
  and the line it must fall below) and the golem gathers itself, falls back down the
  street and builds the next figure farther off; each figure felled is a heavy hammer.
  A figure left standing after the third ball and the golem laughs, and the view turns
  back to the buildings. Drive it back five times and it leaps over the shore road,
  across the beach and into the sea in a great splash; the player goes down to the water
  in the glare, with two more heavy hammers, and comes back to the street.

## 0.38
- The alley behind the player. Before the 51st and the 91st building, and the 151st and
  191st, and so on, and whenever the new blue button in the booth is pressed, the view
  turns round: the street runs on behind to the shore road, with a sea wall, a beach under
  umbrellas and the sea beyond, the last house turning the corner. The golem comes round
  that corner in leaps, stops in the road and falls apart, its stones and bricks flying in
  arcs to stand up as a figure: ten pins in a triangle, a brick wall, a gate, a bending
  row of slabs that topple one another, a stepped pyramid, three ranks of pins, two towers bridged, three totems.
- Bowling: a heavy ball is held low in front; a swipe up rolls it, aimed by where the
  swipe starts and which way it leans, faster for a quicker swipe, rumbling down the
  asphalt. Two balls to a figure; a stone counts once it lies or has been pushed off its
  spot. All down with the first ball is a strike: thunder and a flash. Then the stones fly
  back together into the golem, it leaps, and falls apart into the next figure. Five
  figures in all, shown as five squares in the corner, filled green, amber or grey, a star
  for a strike.
- At the end the gods give up to three heavy hammers for what was knocked down, and the
  view turns back to the building that waits. Nothing is lost in the alley.

## 0.37
- The level plate warns without a word: pale green while buildings fall, and with every
  one in a row that will not, it ripens through amber to red, the number turning white
  and then yellow, as the count nears the tenth that brings the golden hammer or the golem.
- Gold shines now. The golden hammer, in the hand, in flight and as it tumbles from the
  sky, and the golden cube on a building glow with a warm light that hugs their outline
  and breathes, a sheen sweeps across them and sparks twinkle round: no plain yellow
  block looks like that.
- On the hundredth level the button on the lamp post calls the player: it flashes, the
  light spilling round it, and the dispatcher's line rings every few seconds until it is
  pressed.

## 0.36
- No god ever yawns now. When the stars run out the thunderer glowers, the goddess dozes,
  the thinker doubts, and the elder and the idler simply look on.

## 0.35
- The level number in the corner sits on a framed plate of its own, in the street and
  in the fight, so the sky behind it never swallows it.
- From the 80th level the plinth stands farther still: thirty-six meters back, then two
  more every five levels, forty-six at most. The view closes in deeper for the throw, and
  the arm puts more into a throw that long.
- New parts for grown buildings, from the 12th level: old suitcases in brown, mustard and
  burgundy, worn pale at the corners, strapped, with brass latches and loud hotel stickers,
  stacked two or three high on the upper storeys. They soak up a blow and answer with a
  dull leather slap; one sent flying snaps its latches with a thin ding, gapes open and
  lets out a swarm of bright flowered shirts and white postcards that flutter down over
  the street and lie where they fall.
- Spring mattresses in blue-and-white striped ticking with rust stains, pressed between
  two concrete slabs: whatever stands on one sinks into it a little and rocks back upright
  instead of simply tipping over. Struck, a mattress gives a deep whump and a long
  complaining creak of wire; struck hard, its ticking tears, twisted springs jump out of
  the hole and grey wadding puffs out.
- Far away over the street, seldom, a small flock of birds crosses the sky; more seldom
  still an airliner passes high up, leaving a white trail that spreads and fades.

## 0.34
- New parts for grown buildings, from the 10th level: empty steel drums in faded paint
  with rust, that ring when struck; old car tyres stacked into columns, that bounce
  with a dull rubber thump; road cones on the tops; sandbags laid like bricks as heavy
  footings, that land without a ring in a puff of sand.
- No words in the game any more, apart from GOLEM and HOME in the booth. A number the
  telephone does not know answers with three rising beeps, the display blinking dark
  and its dashes shaking. The keys C and CALL are pictures now: an arrow that wipes the
  last digit, and a handset. The sign on the booth's roof is a handset too; the corner
  shows the level as a bare number.

## 0.33
- The golem's world: on the way down from the sky the street turns red and black, every
  color still alive under the filter, and the golem burns in a flickering golden fire.
- A lost fight goes dark; a moment later the player opens his eyes on the street, the
  lids parting and the world blurred, then clear, before the same building.
- A won fight: the pieces of the golem jump about while the ground trembles, then the view
  goes up into the sky, from red through gold to plain day, and every god laughs aloud,
  as on the very first level.
- Level 1 cannot be dialled from the booth any more.

## 0.32
- The farthest level reached is kept when you go back to an earlier one. In 0.31 it
  was saved only when a new level was passed, so going back from far ahead lost it.
- Inside the booth is a telephone now: a green display, a keypad with its own tones,
  CALL and C. Dial the level and call it; a level not yet reached shows NO SUCH LEVEL.
  Below the keypad, the red button calls the golem; HANG UP leads back to the street.

## 0.31
- A call box on the lamp post to the right: a grey case with a red button. Each press
  rings the dispatcher. Press three times and a phone booth opens: inside, a button for
  every level reached, the current one lit, and at the bottom the golem's button. The
  way out is the button marked STREET, or the back key.
- Swapping hammers is easier: a tap on the hammer may last up to six tenths of a second
  and wander a little; before, a slow tap turned into a throw.

## 0.30
- The golem is new: a hunched giant heaped from stone blocks and courses of brick, rods
  of rusty iron out of its shoulders and back, fists of rubble, a stone head sunk between
  the shoulders with a brick jaw and burning eyes. Bricks are laid in courses now.
- It leaps. It crouches, springs six to eight meters down the street and comes down with
  a blow that shakes the screen and throws up dust; it stands a moment, then leaps again,
  more often the nearer it is. A broken knee makes the leaps short; both, and it drags
  itself along on its knees.
- It throws faster and harder the nearer it is. When a car is about to arrive, time runs
  slow, so it can still be knocked away. On the last armor the heartbeat is heard. It
  roars.
- A car in the air shows a black floor, not a painted one.
- It comes only where the second golden hammer would have come; no more every 25th level.

## 0.29
- The golem. A giant put together from what the buildings are made of: stone legs, a
  body of bricks, arms of water bottles, a polished ball for a head. It comes up the
  street from fifty meters off, tears the parked cars from their places and throws them
  at the player, more often the nearer it gets.
- For the fight the gods give a diamond hammer: it flies hard and flat and comes back
  quickly. Knock the cars out of the air; one near the ring is led by the throw. Three
  pieces of armor take a car each; the fourth car, or the golem reaching the player,
  ends the fight.
- Its weak points glow: two knees and the head. One knee broken and it limps; both, and
  it drops to its knees and crawls, its head brought low; three blows to the head and it
  falls apart into bricks, stones, bottles and a ball. A blow to its bulk knocks a brick
  out and stops it for a moment.
- It comes at every 25th level, once, and to a player stuck on one building ten times
  after the golden hammer has already been given (that hammer comes only once). Win and
  the level is cleared with an ovation and three heavy throws; lose and the building is
  there as before.

## 0.28
- From the 50th level the plinth goes farther again: twenty-four meters, two more every
  five levels, thirty at most. The view closes in a little closer for it.
- Whatever falls to the street casts a shadow there: a ball a long soft oval away from
  the sun and a dark spot right under it, a block a patch as wide as it lies; higher up,
  fainter.

## 0.27
- The thinker's newest likeness smirks as well, so it comes on every kind of visit.

## 0.26
- The five newest likenesses have their reactions now: the elder laughs, the thunderer
  laughs and glowers, the goddess smiles and dozes, the idler grins and yawns, the
  thinker raises a brow. They come on any visit to the sky where that face is wanted.

## 0.25
- Five more likenesses of the gods, seen from below. These have one face each, at rest,
  so they come on visits when that god is to keep his face; a god who is to laugh, frown
  or doze comes in a likeness that has that face. More faces, more chance.

## 0.24
- The bearded gods never yawn: when the stars run out the elder keeps his calm face.
- The golden cube: once the golden hammer has been given, now and then (one building in
  four) a golden cube crowns the building. It never falls to the street. Knock the
  building from under it and it comes down onto the plinth and stays there. It does not
  count as weight.

## 0.23
- The golden hammer: lose the same building ten times running and, while the gods
  yawn, a golden hammer tumbles down from the sky. It is in hand when the building comes
  back, and whatever part of the building it touches, the whole of it flies to pieces.
  A miss does not waste it. The count is kept between games and starts again with every
  building cleared.

## 0.22
- Buildings are grown now, not only drawn from recipes. From the 9th level two buildings
  of three are grown storey by storey: posts under a slab, a brick wall, a palisade of
  close posts, a top-heavy deck on two posts, low steps, or a slab carried by something
  that gives way. Towers narrow, shift aside, or split in two, mostly mirrored; twin
  towers of one height may be bridged by a plank and go on above it. Crowns: a cube, a
  little pyramid of three, a balanced T with a weight at each end, a ball, a bottle.
- What carries a slab can give way: water bottles that burst, bowling balls on pads that
  roll out, and balloons. A balloon floats a little, carries a plank while loaded, pops
  at the touch of the hammer or a hard knock, and a freed one drifts up and away.
- Each grown building is tested twice before it is shown: it must stand on its own, and
  one full throw at its middle must knock off about the share this level asks for, from
  three quarters early on to a fifth later. The next building is grown in the background
  while the gods are watching.
- Every third building from the 9th on is still one of the recipes, as a landmark.

## 0.21
- From the 40th level the plinth goes farther still: twelve meters, then two more every
  five levels, twenty at most.
- When the plinth is far, the view closes in on the building while a throw is aimed and
  in flight, and opens out again once the hammer is back in hand. The zoomed view is a
  camera of its own with the street painted for it, not an enlarged picture; the aim
  moves finer in it.
- Parked cars are solid now. The hammer and flying blocks bounce off them and dent
  them: crazed windscreen, dents, broken side glass, a lost headlamp, the roof pressed
  lower, step by step. Each hit crunches, scatters glass and sets off a short alarm with
  blinking lights.

## 0.20
- Bowling balls: heavy, polished, marbled, with three finger holes that turn as they
  roll. The physics knows spheres now, against boxes and against each other.
- Three new buildings from the 15th level, every other building one of them: a pile
  of balls stacked like cannonballs in a stone frame; a beam on stone pillars with a row
  of balls along it; a tower of storeys with a ball on every floor. Each ball sits on a
  small pad between two stops it does not touch, so it stays put until something tips it.
- Fewer stand-ins: a building that only settles by a few centimeters now counts as
  standing, and starts the level already settled. Before, many towers and racks failed
  that test and the first tower stood in for them.

## 0.19
- The plinth's move down the street is now plain to see: three meters at the 21st
  level, two more every five levels after, ten meters at most by the 51st.

## 0.18
- The first building is a welcome. It stands close, where the first plinth of the game
  stood, and flies to pieces at the first touch of the hammer. Every god who can laugh
  laughs aloud, and the heavy hammer comes down as the first gift. The real game starts
  with the second building.

## 0.17
- Only one god opens his mouth wide at a verdict; a sky full of open mouths read as
  wailing. On an ovation it is the thunderer, otherwise one of those who would laugh or
  yawn, at random; the rest keep their quiet face.
- Heavy throws add up: three more come with every ovation, on top of what is left (nine
  at most).
- The gift comes without words: the heavy hammer drops from the thunderer's cloud and
  tumbles down past the gods.
- The other hammer is always in sight, its head at the lower left; a tap on it swaps.
- The plinth moves a full meter farther every five levels from the 21st; four meters at
  most.

## 0.16
- From the 21st level the plinth stands farther down the street, every five levels a
  little more. The cones go with it.

## 0.15
- The gift of the gods: fell a building at one throw and they give the heavy hammer,
  three throws of it, and put it in your hand for the next building. A dark wrought head
  bound in bronze: it winds up slower, leaves the hand slower and tumbles lazily, but
  weighs two and a half times as much.
- Small dark hammers under the stars count the heavy throws left (never more than
  three); they are ringed in gold while the heavy hammer is in hand.
- A short tap on the hammer in hand, or on those marks, puts the heavy hammer down or
  takes it up again. Unused heavy throws are kept between games.

## 0.14
- Strength is a wind-up now, not a flick. Aim, then rest the finger: the hammer draws
  back, a golden band fills round the ring, the arc flattens; let go to throw. A quick
  release lobs, a full wind-up flies hard and flat. The phone gives a short tick when
  the wind-up is full.
- Held past the peak, the arm tires and the ring starts to shake: strength costs
  precision.
- The ring follows every move of the finger again; there is no flick to dodge.

## 0.13
- Aim and strength come apart: a slow drag moves the ring, a quick flick only throws and
  leaves the ring where it was. In 0.12 the flick dragged the ring off the target, so a
  hit came only from a gentle release, and a gentle release is a weak throw.
- Painted clouds hang behind the gods, paler with the distance, fading low down into
  the sky over the street.

## 0.12
- Aiming no longer happens under the finger. The ring starts on the building (or where
  the last throw went) and follows the finger's moves, so the finger can rest anywhere
  on the screen, well away from what it aims at.
- The gods come in more than one likeness now: five new ones seen from below, leaning
  over the edge of their clouds to look down at the street. Each visit to the sky picks
  one likeness for each god.
- The elder takes his place among them.

## 0.11
- The gods are painted now, each in a cloud of his own, with three faces apiece. When
  the view tips up they sail in from the sides, the nearer ones faster, and their faces
  come out of the vapour as they settle.
- They judge with their faces: a wisp of haze drifts over each, and as it clears the
  thunderer laughs or glowers, the goddess smiles or dozes off, the thinker smirks or
  raises a brow, the idler grins or yawns.
- The lightning strikes from behind the thunderer's cloud.
- Behind them: shafts of the low sun and thin rosy streaks of cloud.

## 0.10
- A new sky: towering clouds made of noise, heaped in billows, gold where the low sun
  reaches, soft lavender in the folds, with a fine grain.
- The gods are carved faces half dissolved in the clouds, toned to their light. They
  have no hands any more; they judge with their mouths: laughter (and the thunderer's
  lightning), a smile, nothing, or a yawn.
- The next building comes out of nothing: its pieces appear 15, 10 and 5 meters up and
  fall into place as the view comes back down to the street.

## 0.9
- The audience in the sky are now the old gods of the place, carved of cloud like marble
  and lit gold from below by the low sun: a bearded thunderer, a goddess with a laurel
  wreath, a thinker with his chin on his fist, an elder and one who is easily bored.
- They judge the fall: laughter and three strokes of lightning (with thunder) for one
  throw, smiles for a few, a polite stare for many; when the stars run out, eyes close
  and the bored one yawns behind his hand.
- The gods build the next level: the elder and the thunderer throw blocks down, and as
  the view comes back to the street the new building falls out of the sky into place.
- Towering cumulus banks lit as whole masses: gold on the sun side, lavender in the folds.

## 0.8
- Between buildings the view tips up into the evening sky, where the clouds are the
  audience, with faces you can just make out. One throw: they bounce and burst into puffs
  of applause. Two or three: they cheer. More: they sway politely. Stars run out: one of
  them yawns and drifts away. Meanwhile the next building rises below, and the view comes
  back down to it standing.
- Water bottles turn up in about a third of the buildings (and always on the rack), not in
  every one.

## 0.7
- Big water bottles: see-through blue plastic with ribs and a blue cap, glossy in the sun.
  They stand in towers, crown the seesaw's loads, sit under arches and on the summits.
- A new kind of building: a rack of shelves lined with bottles.
- A bottle bursts at a hard blow or a hard landing: a wet slap, a fan of glittering spray,
  a puff of mist. A burst bottle counts as knocked off.
- Water stays: a puddle spreads where it burst; on the plinth it pours over the front
  edge, runs down the stone in streaks and pools at the foot. Puddles dry in half a minute.

## 0.6
- People are round now: shaded heads, tapered limbs, shorts, trousers or skirts, long
  hair, hats and bags.
- Cars are little sedans with windscreens, side glass, chrome bumpers, lights and plates;
  a row of them is parked along both curbs, and traffic cones stand by the plinth.
- Shadows of people and cars are soft, with blurred edges and fading tails.
- A warm fill light on everything that faces the thrower; a stronger glow on the sun side
  of the sky.

## 0.5
- Evening by the sea. A low sun from the left lays long, dark shadows across the road:
  dappled shadows of palm crowns, the shadow of the plinth and of every piece.
- To the left a promenade with two rows of palms, a stone parapet and the sea glittering
  beyond it; to the right a row of old pastel houses with shutters, iron balconies,
  flower pots and striped awnings over the shops. Lamp posts, bollards, cafe parasols.
- An evening sky, deep blue above and warm by the sun, with streaks of pink cloud.
- Buildings are half as big again, on a bigger granite plinth; the hammer is heavier.
- People and cars in colour, lit by the low sun, each with a long shadow.

## 0.4
- The plinth stands farther off, and the hammer flies a real arc: slower, higher, with a
  pale wake behind its head. The hammer is heavier to make up for it.
- The street keeps to the background: people walk by without noticing the game, cars
  come, turn round and drive back. They are drawn as soft silhouettes with long
  shadows, and make no sound.

## 0.3
- The street is alive: people stroll along both pavements, some in straw hats or dark
  glasses. They stop to watch a throw, jump at a crash and run off.
- Cars come down the road, stop to look and turn round. If what they saw was a ruin,
  they honk, turn with screaming tyres and floor it in a cloud of exhaust.

## 0.2
- The street is seen at a slant: the thrower stands in the left lane.
- The plinth stands farther down the street.

## 0.1
- First playable: the street, the plinth, seven kinds of buildings, the hammer that
  comes back, five stars, the instant retry, synthesized sound.
